The ASI filter: an illustrative model

Will humanity pass the test of artificial superintelligence (ASI) — learn to govern the technology before it gets out of control, and become a spacefaring civilisation?

Humanity is not programmed for self-destruction: the outcome depends on whether the ability to govern technology grows faster than the technology itself. There is no exact formula that gives the answer — none of the quantities can be measured. But the idea of the "great filter" can be written down as a model to see what the outcome depends on.

1. A race between two curves

  • C(t) — the power of technology (AI capabilities);
  • W(t) — humanity's ability to govern it: AI safety science, institutions, international agreements.

Technology grows exponentially at rate α, governance at rate β:

dCdt = α·C,     dWdt = β·W

The danger is determined by the gap between them:

G(t) = C(t) − W(t)

The test comes when the power of technology reaches the superintelligence threshold C*. The condition for passing the filter is that by this moment t* we are able to govern it:

W(t*) ≥ C(t*),   where C(t*) = C*

2. The probability of survival

The risk of catastrophe at each moment is proportional to the gap; λ is the "cost of error":

h(t) = λ · max(0, C(t) − W(t))

The probability of making it all the way to ASI:

Ppass = exp( −λ ∫0t* max(0, C(t) − W(t)) dt )

Survival falls exponentially with the accumulated gap — the area between the technology and governance curves. It matters not only to catch up with technology but also not to lag behind it for long.

3. Breaking it down by conditions

In the spirit of the Drake equation, the probability of becoming a spacefaring civilisation is the product of four conditions:

Pspace = PA · PC · PM · PR
  • PA — alignment: ASI pursues goals compatible with human ones;
  • PC — coordination: countries and companies do not stage a race in which safety is sacrificed for speed;
  • PM — protection from misuse: the technology does not fall into the hands of those who would use it for destruction;
  • PR — resilience: mistakes are correctable and do not become irreversible.

It is a product: the failure of any factor brings the result to zero. The chain breaks at its weakest link.

What the model says

The outcome is set by the parameters, and the parameters depend on people's choices. The filter is passed by a civilisation with β ≥ α: wisdom grows no slower than power.

LeverIn the formulaWhat it means in practice
Slow the growth of powerreduce αpauses, licensing, compute limits
Speed up governanceincrease βsafety research, regulation
Lower the cost of errorreduce λoversight, the ability to "switch off", backup systems
Push back the thresholdincrease t*more time before ASI to prepare

RULE: survival = wisdom growing faster than power.

This is a model for reflection, not a forecast.
