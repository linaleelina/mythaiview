Bangkok Bank's role in Thailand's banking system

Bangkok Bank (ธนาคารกรุงเทพ) is Thailand's largest commercial bank by assets and the most international of the Thai banks. Its overseas network and more than 1,000 correspondent banks make it one of the main channels for transfers into Thailand from abroad.

Its place in the system

  • History. Founded on 1 December 1944. In 1975 it was among the first 16 companies listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand.
  • Size. At the end of 2025: assets 4.61 trillion baht, loans 2.61 trillion baht, deposits 3.20 trillion baht, capital adequacy 21.78%.
  • Systemic importance. Since 2017 the Bank of Thailand has included Bangkok Bank in the list of Domestic Systemically Important Banks (D-SIB), together with Krungthai, Krungsri (Bank of Ayudhya), Kasikornbank and SCB; TTB was added in 2021. Such banks hold additional capital and are under stricter supervision, because their problems would affect the whole economy.

International network

Bangkok Bank has the largest overseas network among Thai banks: it operates in 14 economies. The bank opened its first overseas branch in Hong Kong in 1954 — the first overseas branch of a Southeast Asian bank.

  • ASEAN: Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Vietnam.
  • East Asia: China, Hong Kong, Japan, Taiwan.
  • Financial centres: London and New York.
  • Subsidiary banks abroad: Bangkok Bank Berhad in Malaysia, Bangkok Bank (China) and PermataBank — one of the 10 largest banks in Indonesia.

Why a transfer arrives through Bangkok Bank

Often a transfer to another Thai bank first lands at Bangkok Bank, which then passes the money on to the recipient's bank. Here Bangkok Bank acts as an intermediary bank. This happens in two cases.

  • SWIFT bank transfer. The sender's bank abroad is not always directly connected to the recipient's bank in Thailand. Then the payment goes through an intermediary bank that holds accounts of both parties: it receives the SWIFT instruction, carries out checks, deducts its fee and passes the money on. Bangkok Bank often turns out to be such an intermediary thanks to its network of more than 1,000 correspondent banks and its branches in New York, London, Tokyo and other cities. The money does not physically move: banks debit and credit amounts on the accounts they hold with each other.
  • Transfer service. Wise and similar services pay out money within Thailand through local partner banks, so at the recipient's bank the transfer may not look international. Bangkok Bank is one such partner; a service's partner bank may change.

What this means in practice

  • On your statement the transfer may look domestic — from Bangkok Bank, not from abroad.
  • Ask the intermediary bank for the FET form or Credit Advice. If a Wise transfer went through Bangkok Bank but your account is at another bank, contact Bangkok Bank's head office via the call centre during business hours (in the provinces, the local branch) with your passport and the service's confirmation of the transfer.
  • Each intermediary may deduct a fee, so less than the amount sent arrives in the account.

What to choose for your purpose

The transfer route is chosen by the sender's bank, so the recipient cannot fully avoid an intermediary. But the transfer method can be chosen to fit the purpose.

Deposit insurance

Deposits at Bangkok Bank, as at other banks, are protected by the Deposit Protection Agency (DPA, dpa.or.th), established under a 2008 law. Since 11 August 2021 baht deposits have been protected up to 1 million baht per depositor per financial institution. Protection covers both individuals and legal entities. Foreign currency deposits are not protected.

RULE: the 1 million baht limit applies per depositor per bank. Amounts above the limit can be spread across different banks.

Foreign currency account (FCD) for a foreigner

A non-resident foreigner can open a Foreign Currency Deposit (FCD) account at Bangkok Bank — current, savings or fixed-term. It can be funded by SWIFT transfer, foreign cash, cheques and drafts. Deposits and withdrawals can be made only at the branch where it was opened (an account opened at the head office is serviced at all branches with foreign exchange services). A 15% tax is withheld on interest.
