Business taxes

A resident company is taxed on its worldwide income. Accounts are kept under Thai standards (TFRS) and documents are retained for 5 years.

Corporate Income Tax (CIT)

Losses are carried forward for 5 years. The annual PND 50 return is filed within 150 days of the end of the financial year, the half-year PND 51 within 2 months of the end of the first half. Companies with BOI privileges may be exempt from corporate income tax for 3 to 13 years.

From 2025, groups with consolidated revenue of 750 million euros or more pay a 15% global minimum tax (Global Minimum Tax, Top-up Tax, Pillar Two).

Value Added Tax (VAT)

  • Rate 7% until 30.09.2027. The statutory rate is 10%; it is reduced every year by royal decree.
  • Registration is mandatory if revenue exceeds 1.8 million baht a year.
  • Exports are zero-rated. VAT-exempt: property rental, healthcare, education, sale of unprocessed agricultural produce.
  • The PP 30 return is filed monthly by the 15th (online by the 23rd).

Specific Business Tax (SBT)

3.3% of revenue (3% plus 0.3% local tax) instead of VAT for banks, finance companies, insurers, pawnshops and commercial sales of real estate.

Withholding Tax (WHT)

Who withholds tax

The duty to withhold tax (ผู้มีหน้าที่หักภาษี ณ ที่จ่าย) lies with whoever pays the income and depends on who pays whom and for what:

  • Employer (a company or an individual): income tax on salary according to the scale, form PND 1, 50 Tawi certificate.
  • A company or juristic partnership paying a resident for services, rent, advertising or transport: at the rates in the table below. Form PND 3 if the recipient is an individual, PND 53 if a company.
  • A payer to abroad withholds tax on payments to a foreign company without a presence in Thailand (form PND 54) and self-assesses 7% VAT on services from a foreigner (form PP 36).
  • Companies and banks paying dividends and interest to individuals: form PND 2.
  • Government agencies: 1% on all payments to suppliers and contractors.
  • Department of Lands: withholds the seller's tax when a property transaction is registered.

A private individual who does not run a business usually does not withhold tax. Withheld tax is most often not final: the recipient includes the income in their return and credits what was withheld.

Double taxation agreements: nuances

  • Services. If under the agreement payment for services counts as business profits and the foreign company has no permanent establishment in Thailand, no tax is withheld at all. But in some agreements consulting and technical services count as royalties, and then tax is withheld at the treaty rate.
  • Dividends. The Thai 10% rate is not reduced by treaties. If the treaty rate is higher, the Thai 10% applies.
  • Interest. Usually 10% or 15% depending on the recipient (banks and financial institutions often get a lower rate).
  • Documents. The payer applies the treaty benefit only with the recipient's certificate of tax residence. The recipient must be the beneficial owner of the income.
  • If tax was withheld at the full rate, the overpayment can be reclaimed by application on form Kor. 10 (ค.10) within 3 years of the filing deadline.
  • VAT still applies. Even without income tax withholding, the payer self-assesses 7% VAT on a foreigner's services (form PP 36).

Forms PND 3, 53 and 54 are filed by the 7th of the following month (online by the 15th).

Petroleum Income Tax

A separate tax under the Petroleum Income Tax Act B.E. 2514 (1971). It is paid by companies exploring for and producing oil and gas in Thailand under a concession or production sharing contract (PSC), and by those who buy oil from them for export. The ordinary 20% corporate income tax does not apply to this activity. Remittance of profits out of Thailand is also taxed.

Apart from the tax, concessionaires pay royalty (from 5% to 15% depending on production volume) and a Special Remuneratory Benefit (SRB) at a progressive rate from 0% to 75% on windfall profit. The tax is administered by the Revenue Department, royalty and SRB by the Department of Mineral Fuels.

This tax concerns only oil and gas companies. For individuals, fuel trading and petrol stations the ordinary taxes apply: corporate income tax, VAT and excise.

For employers

  • An employer must withhold tax on salary monthly (form PND 1), file the annual PND 1 Kor report and issue employees a 50 Tawi certificate. This also applies to an individual employer, for example if you hire domestic staff with a salary above the tax-free threshold.
  • Social Security Fund (SSF): 5% from the employee and 5% from the employer on wages up to 17,500 baht in 2026–2028 (a maximum of 875 + 875 baht a month). From 2029 the base rises to 20,000, from 2032 to 23,000.

Minimum Wage

In Thailand the minimum wage is set per day and separately by province (Wage Committee Notification No. 14, in force since 1 July 2025). There were no changes in 2026. The minimum wage applies to all private-sector workers, including foreigners and, since April 2024, domestic staff.

For a worker on a monthly salary at a rate of 400 baht, the minimum is 12,000 baht a month (400 × 30).

Working hours

  • The legal working week is up to 6 days. The norm is no more than 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week (Labour Protection Act B.E. 2541, Section 23). For hazardous work, 7 hours a day and 42 hours a week.
  • At least 1 day off a week, with no more than 6 working days in a row between days off. Many offices and international companies work 5 days of 8 hours, but that is a contract term, not a legal requirement.
  • A break of at least 1 hour after 5 hours of work.
  • Overtime only with the employee's consent; together with work on days off, no more than 36 hours a week. Pay: 1.5× on a working day, 2× for work on a day off or holiday, 3× for overtime on a day off or holiday.
  • Public holidays: at least 13 paid days a year, including 1 May. The employer announces the list in advance.
  • Annual leave: at least 6 working days after a year of service. Sick leave: up to 30 paid days a year.

Stamp Duty

Paid on contracts: lease 0.1%, loans 0.05% (no more than 10,000 baht), share transfer 0.1%, property transfer 0.5%.
