Consumption

MY VIEW: consuming less means understanding better what is an expense and what is an investment. Your attention is being managed, but the decision to buy remains yours.

— Lina Lee

How the consumer economy works

A consumer economy is a model in which the main engine of growth is people's spending on goods and services. It works as a closed loop:

  1. A person works and receives wages.
  2. Spends them on purchases.
  3. Businesses earn revenue, hire people and pay wages.
  4. New wages turn into purchases again.

To make the loop spin faster, credit is added: a person buys today with money they will earn tomorrow. And advertising, fashion and the short lifespan of things push people to buy more often than they need. The state has an interest in this loop: it collects taxes on every purchase and every wage.

The most striking example is the US: household spending there accounts for about 68% of GDP. When Americans spend less, the whole world economy feels it, including Thailand's tourism and exports.

  1. Consumption rests on a narrow group of people. According to Moody's Analytics, the wealthiest 10% of Americans account for 45.5% of all spending, and the top 20% for about 60%. Thirty years ago the top 10%'s share was about 36%. The wealthy spend partly out of stock market gains: if the market falls, spending will drop sharply, and the remaining 80–90% of the population have nothing to pick it up with.
  2. The loop runs on debt. US household debt has risen to a record 19.1 trillion dollars, and credit card debt to a record 1.29 trillion. Since the end of 2019 debt has grown by about 5 trillion. New York Fed researchers say plainly that the growth in debt reflects financial hardship.
  3. People are spending more than they can afford. To maintain their usual standard of living as prices rise, Americans are saving less and less: in September 2026, 3.8% of income — less than four cents of every dollar.
  4. Thailand has the same problem. Household debt is about 85–87% of GDP. People increasingly borrow for everyday expenses. The debt-to-GDP ratio is falling, but not because people have become richer: banks have simply stopped lending, and people turn to cooperatives and pawnshops. The Bank of Thailand believes high debt is preventing the economy from returning to normal growth.
  5. The "work → wages → purchases" link is weakening. Automation and AI make it possible to produce more with fewer workers. If wages stop growing along with production, there will be no one to buy the output. More on the "The future economy" page.

Conclusion: the consumer economy is still growing, but it rests on debt, dwindling savings and the spending of a small well-off group. This is not a sustainable flow of money but a flow into debt.

People and the attention economy

Consumption begins not in the shop but with our attention. Advertising, social networks and marketplace algorithms study people's interests and suggest what to buy. Discounts, sales and instalment plans can create a sense of a good deal and push people towards a purchase that would not have happened without the offer.

AI amplifies this influence: it talks to a person like a personal adviser, answers questions and helps work through doubts. Such a dialogue can be useful, but it can also quietly steer a person towards a purchase.

That is why it is important to understand where your income goes: on necessary expenses, impulse purchases or investment in the future. The decision to buy should remain with the person — conscious and informed.

A thought experiment: who goes broke first

You have lost your job for 5 yearsImagine: you are out of work and cannot find a new job for five years. What will you do? First you will spend your savings, and then you will start selling what you have: the car, electronics, jewellery, perhaps the apartment. That is when it becomes clear which of your purchases were investments and which were expenses: some things can be sold, for others no one will give you anything.
You have stopped consuming right nowNow the other way round: you stop going to the cinema, the gym and restaurants, you don't buy yet another pair of shoes, a new phone or another subscription. It is not you who goes broke first, but the businesses that live on your spending. Your money stays with you.

The consumer economy is set up so that the buyer carries the risk: they spend today and pay for it later — with debt, a lack of savings, selling off property at a difficult moment. By no longer spending on what you don't need, you shift that risk back onto business.

How to stop being a resource and become an owner

The modern economy is set up so that a person is at once a worker, a consumer, a source of data and an object of attention. Companies earn money on each of these resources.

But a person can change their role: not only creating profit for others but also sharing in its distribution. To do this, it is important to act on five levels.

1. Manage your attention

Your attention is an economic resource. The more time you spend on platforms, in shops and on social networks, the more opportunities companies have to make money on you.

  • Turn off unnecessary notifications.
  • Unsubscribe from advertising mailings.
  • Do not make purchase decisions under the pressure of urgency and artificial scarcity.
  • Use a pause rule before unplanned purchases.
  • Do not let algorithms define your desires.

2. Manage your money

Every purchase is a decision about who gets your income and which economic activity you support.

  • Avoid consumer loans for non-essential purchases.
  • Do not overpay for convenience if it has no real value to you.
  • Compare prices and purchase terms.
  • Support small and local businesses where this is in your interest.
  • Build savings and a financial reserve.

3. Move from consumption to ownership

This is the next level of economic independence.

As long as you only buy goods and services, you take part in creating companies' profits as a consumer. But you can also become an owner of part of a business.

Shares, bonds, stakes in companies and index funds let you take part in the economy not only through consumption but also through owning capital¹.

In this way a person can remain a buyer of a company's products and at the same time gain economic benefit from owning a part of it.

4. Protect your rights and data

Your personal data, digital activity and consumer rights have economic value.

  • Control your consents to the processing of personal data.
  • Limit the sharing of information that is not needed to receive a service.
  • Use your right to opt out of the advertising use of your data.
  • Insist on warranties and consumer rights being respected.
  • Leave honest reviews and report violations.

5. Take part in shaping the rules of the economy

An individual can manage their own behaviour, but market rules are shaped collectively.

Competition, antitrust regulation, personal data protection, advertising transparency and consumer rights determine how fairly economic opportunities are distributed.

Taking part in civic initiatives, supporting transparent businesses and making informed choices as a citizen allow you to influence these rules.

THE MAIN PRINCIPLE: do not limit yourself to the role of consumer. Become an owner of assets, manage your resources, protect your rights and take part in economic processes.

Your goal is not simply to give less to corporations, but to build your own economic resilience and share in the distribution of the value created.

Anti-marketing: business without manipulation²

Marketing seeks to influence a person's decision. Anti-marketing offers a different model: not to manage the consumer's desires, but to earn their trust.

In the traditional marketing model, a business's task is to stimulate demand, attract attention and turn it into sales. Advertising, personalised offers, artificial urgency, emotional triggers and the creation of new needs are used for this.

Anti-marketing questions the very need for these tools when they limit a person's freedom of choice.

How it works in practice

Imagine a developer selling villas in Phuket.

Conventional marketing says:

  • Luxury living by the sea!
  • High investment returns!
  • Last chance to buy at a great price!

Anti-marketing says:

  • The beach is 20 minutes away by car.
  • Some areas may flood in the rainy season.
  • The yield depends on occupancy, management costs and competition.
  • If you are looking for a home within walking distance of the sea, this project probably will not suit you.

The second model may lead to fewer enquiries, but it helps build more informed demand and long-term trust.

Principles of anti-marketing

  1. Do not create artificial needs.
  2. Do not use the fear of missing out to pressure the buyer.
  3. Do not hide the product's drawbacks.
  4. Do not promise results that cannot be guaranteed.
  5. Do not sell a person something that does not suit them.
  6. Respect a person's right to decline a purchase.

It is not just ethics. In Thailand advertising is regulated by the Consumer Protection Act B.E. 2522 (1979). Section 22 prohibits false or exaggerated statements, as well as statements that mislead about the essential qualities of goods or services. Enforcement is carried out by the Office of the Consumer Protection Board (สคบ., OCPB). In 2025 it warned bloggers and online sellers that phrases such as "record sales" or "sold out instantly" may be treated as false advertising. So in Thailand principles 2 and 4 are not only a matter of ethics but also a legal requirement.

Who benefits?

The buyer, because they decide understanding both the pros and the cons, and less often make purchases they later regret. The business, because in the long run this model reduces reputational risks, builds loyalty and bases customer relationships on trust.

The business earns from the value of its product, not from its skill at managing consumer behaviour.

How a buyer can recognise manipulation and protect themselves is covered on the "How to make a decision" page.

THE MAIN PRINCIPLE OF ANTI-MARKETING: not to persuade a person to buy what they do not need, but to give them the chance to understand for themselves the value of what you offer.

RULE: every time before you spend 1 dollar, ask yourself: is this an investment or an unnecessary expense?

¹ The securities market is a separate risk. Prices of shares and fund units can fall, sometimes for a long time; companies can go bankrupt, and bond issuers can fail to repay their debts. Investments can make a loss, up to losing all the money invested. Invest only money you will not need in the coming years, after you have built a financial reserve, and spread it across different assets. This is not an investment recommendation.

² In the context of this site, anti-marketing is a model of the relationship between a business and a buyer without manipulation: honest information about a product's advantages and drawbacks, time to decide and respect for a person's right not to buy. It is not the "demarketing" or "countermarketing" of marketing textbooks (P. Kotler), which is about reducing demand for scarce or harmful goods.
