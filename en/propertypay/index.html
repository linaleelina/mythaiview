Property payments

How a foreigner pays for an apartment in Thailand: where the money must come from, in what currency, which document confirms the transfer, and which of six ways of paying is cheapest.

Transferring money from abroad and the FET form

To register an apartment freehold, a foreigner must show that the money came from abroad in foreign currency. For this the bank issues a Foreign Exchange Transaction Form (FET). Bangkok Bank accepts such transfers via SWIFT into a baht account or a foreign currency account.

Transfers through services like Wise are paid out within Thailand through a local partner, so formally they do not count as international, and the service itself cannot issue an FET form. You have to request it from the partner bank the money came through.

Why a Thai account has no IBAN

IBAN (International Bank Account Number) is an international account number used mainly in Europe and the Middle East. Thailand is not part of the IBAN system, so Thai accounts have no IBAN. Bangkok Bank states plainly that it has no IBAN and the sender does not need to provide one; what is needed is the bank's SWIFT code.

To send money to Thailand, the sender needs:

  • the account holder's name — exactly as at the bank;
  • the account number — digits only, without spaces or hyphens (the length varies between banks);
  • the bank's name and SWIFT code, for example BKKBTHBK for Bangkok Bank, KASITHBK for Kasikornbank, SICOTHBK for SCB, AYUDTHBK for Krungsri;
  • the payment purpose — for example, purchase of an apartment with its number and the project name.

If your bank will not process a transfer without an IBAN, enter the Thai account number in that field or leave it empty if the bank allows, and be sure to give the SWIFT code. If that does not work, ask your bank how to send a transfer to a country without IBAN.

Payment chains: from your account to the developer

Six ways a foreigner with an account abroad can pay a Thai developer for an apartment, from cheapest to most expensive. Each shows whether it works for registering an apartment freehold.

The ranking is based on the typical cost structure: how many times currency is converted, how many banks are involved and who sets the rate. The exact amount depends on your bank, the sum and the currency. The only officially known figure is Thai banks' fee for receiving a SWIFT transfer: 0.25%, no more than 500 baht. ● — relative cost; blue blocks are steps in Thailand.

1
Transfer service (Wise and similar) in baht
Your account abroad → Transfer service → Thai partner bank → Your account in Thailand → Developer's account
Service fee + conversion at a rate close to the market rate. Thai banks charge nothing for the incoming transfer: the money arrives as a domestic transfer.
●○○○○○For freehold: uncertain
Usually the cheapest way, but the transfer arrives in baht and as a domestic one. To register an apartment freehold you need a certificate from the partner bank that the money came from abroad, and it is not always issued.
2
SWIFT in foreign currency directly to the developer's account
Your account abroad → Correspondent bank (if needed) → Developer's bank
Your bank's transfer fee + intermediary fees (if any) + 0.25% for receipt at the Thai bank, no more than 500 baht + one conversion into baht at the Thai bank's rate.
●●○○○○For freehold: yes, FET form
The shortest route among those suitable for freehold: one conversion, one recipient. In the payment purpose give your name and the purpose — purchase of an apartment with its number — so the FET form is issued in your name.
3
SWIFT in foreign currency to your own account in Thailand, then to the developer
Your account abroad → Correspondent bank (if needed) → Your account in Thailand → Developer's account
The same as option 2, plus a transfer within Thailand: via PromptPay or ITMX usually free within limits; large sums by bank cheque or via BAHTNET for a separate fee.
●●●○○○For freehold: yes, FET form
Slightly more expensive and slower than option 2, but the money stays under your control until each payment to the developer — convenient when paying in instalments. The FET form is issued by your bank.
4
SWIFT, but the sender's bank converts the currency
Your account abroad → Conversion into baht abroad → Correspondent bank → Developer's bank
The same fees as option 2, but the rate for conversion into baht is set by the foreign bank — usually worse than the Thai bank's rate.
●●●●○○For freehold: no — the money did not arrive in foreign currency
A common mistake: the transfer goes out in baht straight away. For freehold a foreigner must bring the money in foreign currency, so when sending choose the transfer currency — dollars, euros or another — not baht.
5
Paying by bank card
Your card → Card network → Developer's acquirer → Developer's account
Your bank's fee for a transaction abroad and the card network's conversion; the developer may add a card acceptance fee.
●●●●●○For freehold: no
Suitable only for a small reservation. A large sum is not paid by card: it is expensive, there are limits and there will be no FET form.
6
Foreign cash, cryptocurrency or P2P exchange
Cash, crypto or P2P → Exchange in Thailand → Developer's account
The exchange office's or exchange's rate; bringing in more than 20,000 dollars in cash requires a customs declaration. Paying a developer in cryptocurrency is prohibited.
●●●●●●For freehold: no
The most expensive and risky route: it is not a transfer from abroad, there is no FET form, and large amounts of cash and cryptocurrency attract the attention of banks and regulators. With a P2P exchange the baht come from a stranger's account — if it is fraudsters' money, your account may be frozen (more under P2P exchange).

If relatives send the money

Relatives, a spouse or your company can also pay for the apartment. The law does not prohibit this, but the transfer must be clearly linked to the buyer.

+
SWIFT in foreign currency from a relative's account
Relative's account abroad → Correspondent bank (if needed) → Your account in Thailand or the developer's bank
Cost as in options 2 and 3. Payment purpose: "Purchase of condominium unit No. … in … by [your name as in your passport]".
For freehold: yes, if the buyer is named in the transfer
It is best for the recipient to be the buyer themselves — into their Thai account. Land offices take different approaches: some accept a transfer from a third party with the correct payment purpose, others require the buyer's name to appear on the FET form as sender or recipient. Check in advance with the bank and with a lawyer or the developer.
  • There is no limit on the amount. The 50,000-dollar threshold per transfer determines only the document: from that amount the bank issues an FET form; below it, a credit advice and a confirmation letter from the bank. The Department of Lands accepts both.
  • Do not split the transfer to get around the threshold: there will be more documents to reconcile, and more questions.
  • Keep documents showing your relationship with the sender: a gift letter, a marriage certificate or a company resolution.

What to choose

If the apartment is being registered freehold, choose option 2 or 3: the cheapest way that reliably gives an FET form. Option 1 is cheaper but riskier for freehold. Options 4–6 are not suitable for a foreigner buying an apartment.

Rules

RULE: transfer money for an apartment from abroad in foreign currency via SWIFT directly to a Thai bank, and convert it into baht at the Thai bank. In the payment purpose give your name and the purpose — purchase of an apartment. Ask for the FET form immediately after the money is credited: without it a foreigner cannot register freehold.

RULE: so that a transfer from abroad is visible as international, open an account at a bank that receives money directly via SWIFT, and give its code. Then the FET form is issued by your own bank.

Why a transfer may arrive via an intermediary bank and look like a domestic one is explained on the "Bangkok Bank" page. Fees and taxes on purchase are on the "Buying and fees" page. Which payments the bank and the land office report to AMLO is on the "AMLO" page.
