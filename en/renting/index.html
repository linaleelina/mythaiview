Renting out property

Short-term and long-term rentals, rental pool programmes and tax on rental income for residents and non-residents.

Short-term rental: what an owner needs to know

  1. Seasonality. Most income comes in the high season, roughly November to April. In the rainy season (May–October) occupancy and prices drop noticeably, and in some months the unit may stand empty. Every season is different: it depends on tourist arrivals, exchange rates and flight connections.
  2. Look at RevPAR, not the nightly rate. The hotel business measures income with three indicators: occupancy, the average price of a night sold (ADR, Average Daily Rate) and revenue per available night (RevPAR = ADR × occupancy). A high nightly rate with low occupancy earns less than a moderate rate with high occupancy. Hotels constantly manage this balance: they change prices daily depending on demand, raise prices and set minimum stays in the high season, and lower prices and attract long stays in the low season. Ask the management company for monthly reports on occupancy, ADR and RevPAR for your unit or similar units in the building for past years. If it does not keep or provide such data, that is a warning sign.
  3. Costs do not depend on occupancy. The common area fee, utilities, electricity at the management company's rates, insurance, land and building tax and regular repairs are charged all year round. In the low season they may exceed rental income. Calculate the annual yield, not the income for a good month.
  4. The management company's terms. Read the contract carefully: what share of income it takes, whether there is a minimum monthly fee regardless of occupancy, who pays for cleaning, linen and repairs, how many days a year you can stay in the unit yourself and how to terminate the contract. A "guaranteed yield" usually lasts only a few years, after which the market sets the income.
  5. Wear and tear. With daily rentals, the flow of guests and the tropical climate wear out finishes, furniture and appliances faster. Build refurbishment into your calculations.

Rental pool programmes

A rental pool and hotel-type apartments are different things. A hotel licence is a status of the building: it decides whether it is legal to rent out daily. A rental pool is a scheme under a contract with an operator: it decides how income is split among owners. The words "there is a rental programme" do not mean the building has a hotel licence: that must be checked separately. A pool without a hotel licence can legally operate only with rentals of 30 days or more.

How a pool works. Owners hand their apartments to an operator — often a hotel brand or a company related to the developer. The operator rents out all the apartments as a single hotel, income goes into a common pot, costs are deducted, and the remainder is shared among owners in proportion to the area or "points" of each apartment — regardless of how many nights yours was rented. The pluses: income is smoothed and professionals handle management. There is no special law on rental pools: everything is determined by the contract.

What to look for in a pool contract:

  1. Deductions before the split. The operator's fee, marketing, staff, utilities, contributions to the furniture, fixtures and equipment reserve (FF&E reserve) — which costs are deducted and how much.
  2. Transparency. Whether owners have the right to audited accounts and an independent review.
  3. Conflict of interest. If the operator is related to the developer, owners usually cannot replace it.
  4. Own use. How many days a year you can stay in your apartment and in which season.
  5. Mandatory spending. Repairs and replacement of furniture to the operator's standards at your expense.
  6. Term and exit. How many years the contract runs, whether you can leave early and whether a future buyer must stay in the pool.
  7. Transition after the "guarantee". Often a guaranteed yield applies for the first years, after which the apartment automatically moves into the pool and income starts to depend on the market.

RULE: before buying, ask the operator for the pool contract and reports for past years: how much owners actually received per square metre after all deductions. Yield promises without such data are not an argument.

RULE: if you bought a unit that is not in hotel-type apartments (with a hotel licence), the best alternative is long-term rental for a month or longer. It is legal, and the income from it is more stable and less dependent on the season.

Tax on rental income

Income from renting out housing is subject to personal income tax on the ordinary progressive scale (Section 40 (5) of the Revenue Code). It must be paid whether or not you live in Thailand: income from property in Thailand is Thai-source income. But the way tax is withheld depends on whether you are a Thai tax resident, that is whether you spent 180 days or more in the country in the calendar year.

  1. How to reduce the tax. Before calculating tax, expenses can be deducted from the rent. There are two options: deduct 30% of income without documents (the standard deduction) or deduct actual expenses if supported by documents.
  2. You are a resident, the tenant is a company. With each payment it withholds 5% of the rent itself (Withholding Tax) and pays it to the budget. It gives you a withholding certificate on form 50 Tawi (50 ทวิ). This tax is not final: in your annual return you calculate tax on the full rent and deduct what has already been withheld. If too much was withheld, the difference can be refunded.
  3. You are a resident, the tenant is an individual. They withhold nothing, and you pay the tax yourself by filing two returns:
    • PND 94 (ภ.ง.ด.94) — for the first half-year (January–June), by the end of September;
    • PND 90 (ภ.ง.ด.90) — for the whole year, by the end of March of the following year. Tax paid with PND 94 is credited.
  4. You are a non-resident. Anyone who pays you rent — a corporate tenant, an individual tenant or a management company under a rental programme — must withhold 15% of the full rent (Section 50 (3) of the Revenue Code) and pay it to the budget. If the payer did not withhold the tax, they are jointly liable with you for paying it (Section 54). The 15% withheld is not a final tax: through the annual PND 90 return the tax can be recalculated on the scale with expenses deducted and the overpayment refunded. For this you need a Thai Tax ID. Double taxation agreements do not reduce the rate: income from property is taxed in the country where the property is located.
  5. Stamp Duty. On a lease agreement, 0.1% of the total rent for the whole term of the contract is paid (1 baht for every 1,000 baht).
