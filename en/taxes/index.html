Primer: how Thai tax law works

Where the rules come from

  1. Constitution: a tax can only be introduced by law.
  2. Revenue Code (ประมวลรัษฎากร) of 1938 is the main law. It covers personal income tax, corporate income tax, VAT, specific business tax and stamp duty.
  3. Separate laws cover petroleum income, land and building tax, signboard tax, excise, customs and inheritance tax.
  4. Royal Decrees (พระราชกฤษฎีกา) reduce rates and grant exemptions. For example, they extend VAT at 7% instead of the statutory 10% every year.
  5. Ministerial Regulations (กฎกระทรวง) specify deductions and exemptions.
  6. Revenue Department orders and notifications (Departmental Order "Por.", Notification) explain how to apply the law. It was Order Por. 161/2566 that changed the taxation of foreign income from 2024.
  7. Rulings (ข้อหารือ) are clarifications on specific situations. They are guidance, not precedent.

Who collects taxes

  • Revenue Department of the Ministry of Finance: personal income tax, corporate income tax, VAT, specific business tax, stamp duty, petroleum income tax.
  • Excise Department: excise on fuel, alcohol, tobacco and cars.
  • Customs Department: import and export duties.
  • Department of Lands: collects fees and taxes when property transactions are registered.
  • Local administrations: land and building tax, signboard tax.

What you need to know

  • A document takes effect only after publication in the Royal Gazette. Announcements and drafts are not yet law.
  • The official language is Thai. English translations on the Revenue Department website are for reference only.
  • Dates follow the Buddhist calendar: year 2569 = 2026 (a difference of 543 years).
  • Taxpayers calculate and file their own tax. The Revenue Department can audit and issue an additional assessment.
  • An assessment can be appealed to the Board of Appeal and then to the Central Tax Court.

What matters in 2026

The "two-year window" for foreign income has not been adopted. In 2025 the Revenue Department proposed exempting foreign income if it is brought into Thailand in the year it is earned or the following year. As of October 2026 the document has not been published in the Royal Gazette, so the 2024 rule applies.

VAT stays at 7%. Royal Decree No. 807 extended the reduced rate from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027.

The Social Security Fund wage base has increased. From 1 January 2026 the 5% contributions are calculated on wages up to 17,500 baht, a maximum of 875 baht a month from the employee and the same from the employer.
